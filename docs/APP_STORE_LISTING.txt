DESCRIPTION (4000 characters max)
================================================================================

Some arguments go in circles. You have both made the same point four times, you
both feel unheard, and neither of you can say what you are actually disagreeing
about any more.

Dispute is for that. One phone, two people, about fifteen minutes.

HOW IT WORKS

1. Say what the argument is, in one line, and put both your names in.

2. Each of you takes the phone and writes your side properly, on your own, with
the other person not reading over your shoulder.

3. The app turns both sides into one list of points, shuffled together, with no
names on it. You each go through the same list and tick what you agree with.
Because you cannot tell whose point is whose, you end up judging the point.

4. It shows you what you already agree on. In most arguments this is more than
either person expected.

5. Then it names the crux: the one question where, depending on the answer,
either of you would change your mind. Not a summary, not a compromise, and not a
verdict. One question, and something you could actually go and do to settle it.

6. At the end you get the whole thing written up, which you can keep or share.

WHAT IT WILL NOT DO

It never takes a side. It does not tell you who is right, it does not score the
argument, and it does not comment on your relationship. It has no opinion about
either of you and it is not supposed to.

WHAT IT COSTS

Nothing. There is no account, no sign-in, no subscription and no ads. You do not
need to make an account with anyone, and there is nothing to set up before you
start.

THE AI, AND WHAT LEAVES YOUR PHONE

The list of points and the crux are written by an AI, and the first screen
asks whether you want that before anything happens. The AI is off until you say
yes.

Say yes and what you both type is sent away to be read by an AI model, run by
Mistral or by Cloudflare. It costs you nothing, and neither of them uses it to
train anything. The app tells you all of this on that same first screen, before
you type anything.

Say no and the app still works: you write your own points, you name the crux
together from a filled-in draft, and nothing leaves the phone. That is a complete
way to use it and it is how the app started. You can change your mind either way
in Settings.

The one argument you have open is stored in one file on your phone. Deleting it
in Settings deletes it. There is no cloud copy, no sync, and nothing on our end
with your name on it, because there is no account to put a name in.

================================================================================
KEEP THESE TRUE
================================================================================

Five claims in the description are statements about the build, and the listing is
what Apple checks the app against:

1. "It never takes a side."
2. "There is no cloud copy... nothing on our end with your name on it."
3. "Say no and the app still works... nothing leaves the phone."
4. "sent to Mistral, a French AI company" — the provider, by name.
5. "it is not used to train anything" — true only while "Allow your interactions
   to be used to train our models" is off in the Mistral console. It is an
   account setting, not a property of the tier, and it can be turned back on by
   anybody with access to that account.

All five are true today.

[4 and 5 are on this list because they are the two that broke. The provider moved
in 1.1 and this file was updated; the live listing was not, and for a month the
product page told people their arguments went to Google and might be used to
improve Google's products. Neither had been true since the move.

The lesson is narrow and worth writing down: `CloudEngine.providerName` made the
*app* impossible to get wrong, and did nothing for the six fields that live in
App Store Connect. Those are copies, they are not compiled, and nothing fails
when they drift. Changing the provider means editing them by hand, in the same
week, and checking the live page afterwards:

    curl -s "https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?bundleId=com.jamesgpuckett.Dispute" \
      | python3 -c "import json,sys; print(json.load(sys.stdin)['results'][0]['description'])"

That prints what is actually on sale, which is the only version of this text that
matters.]
